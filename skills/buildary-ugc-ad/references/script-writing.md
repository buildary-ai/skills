# Writing the recommendation

The aim is useful, engaging advice from a particular person to a particular listener. Conversational wording
cannot rescue a script with no reason to recommend the product.

## Keep the persuasive chain; make it human

Claim, problem or question, proof, offer and call to action can stay the underlying logic. Express them
through a person's point of view, a recognisable situation, a useful observation and an activity worth
watching, not as five separate slogans. Plan the functions privately; write one coherent conversation.

## Find the reason this person shares it

Start from the viewer's situation: what they are trying to do, the small snag, what would make that moment
better. Choose only a situation the product can credibly address; do not invent drama when curiosity, taste
or convenience is the real motive. Give the speaker a point of view: why this detail interests them and why
they bring it up now. Audience fit comes from recognisable priorities and vocabulary, not stereotypes.

Before drafting, be able to say privately what makes the recommendation worth hearing without the brand
name, the discount or generic praise. "It's great / a game changer" means the reasoning is still missing.

## Turn a fact into a reason

Connect a supported detail → what someone can do or see with it → why it matters in that person's day.
Do not explain what the action already shows; spend words on the reason, judgment or context the image
cannot carry. Specific beats superlative: a colour chosen for someone, a control easier to reach mid-task.
Include a real limitation only when supported; never invent a flaw to sound honest.

## Ground life detail without inventing a life

A fictional creator can have taste, intention or a conditional preference ("if you always lose your keys in
your bag, this pocket…"), but not a purchase, months of use, a medical improvement or a comparison test.

## The first 3-5 seconds

Decide why this viewer keeps watching. Start with a recognisable situation, a pointed question, real
curiosity, a supported contrast, a visible demonstration or a consequential detail. Avoid greetings, generic
hype, unrelated shock and a feature or discount list that delays the reason to care.

The product is recognisable from the first frame: already in hand, in use, or clearly present where it
works. No several seconds of empty-handed talk before fetching it. The brand name can come later. Words and
action work together: the image gives context, the words raise the question or explain the consequence.

| Product | Opening example | Obligation in the body |
|---|---|---|
| Serum | "Does your skincare pill under sunscreen too?" | Creator already holds the serum; answer with a supported explanation, not a claimed result |
| Milk frother | "How do you get that foam on an iced latte at home?" | Frother and glass visible as it starts; show the documented operation |
| Notes app | "Are your meeting notes in three different places?" | Show the real interface; demonstrate the documented flow |

A hook creates an expectation the body must pay off. Curiosity never licenses invented history, results,
superiority, scarcity or testimonials. Do not copy a reference creator's catchphrases.

## Structure and pacing

- Let the viewer follow a thought: each sentence establishes a need, notices a detail, explains it,
  answers a likely question or helps them act.
- An offer can come early when it explains a decision now; it never replaces the reason the product matters.
  Keep every required call to action, code, price or guarantee exactly.
- Estimate length by reading the dialogue aloud at the intended pace, with handling, pauses and reactions.
  About 2.5 words per second is a rough check, not a target. Leave a short natural ending, not a long hold.
- Shorter version: fewer reasons, each still connected. Longer version: more substance, not slower slogans.
- If required wording and meaningful action cannot fit the model's longest duration, say so and offer a
  choice; never rush or drop locked words silently.

## Check the speech on its own

Before any generation, read the dialogue without performance notes. Is there a specific listener need, a
supported detail and why it matters? Watch for interchangeable claims, a shuffleable feature list, invented
life history and offer lines replacing the argument.
