## Start from one idea

Name the impression the audience should keep and a product-specific reason for it. Choose a few visual
elements and relationships (shape, scale, texture, colour, motion, space, behaviour); the product belongs
among them from the start. If an unrelated product could replace yours without changing the treatment, make
the idea more specific. "Premium" or "cinematic" is not a decision; composition, light, material and sound are.

## Choose the expressive form

Pick or combine, according to the brief:

- **Product / sensory:** verified material, mechanism, texture or sound carries the film.
- **Motion / scale:** the whole product, space, speed and camera relationship.
- **Human / editorial:** posture, clothing, action and place as composition.
- **Life / narrative:** a short human situation, when it advances the idea.
- **Conceptual:** one coherent metaphor or visual rule tied to the product.

No actor has to speak; a playful everyday treatment can be a complete commercial. Premium can be bright,
forceful or funny; slow motion, black and gold, shallow focus and expensive architecture are not defaults.

## Make each image concrete

For each key image decide: subject position and size, orientation, foreground and background, negative
space, light-dark balance, colour emphasis and what moves. Allow asymmetry, depth, occlusion and scale
changes. A camera move has a start relation, a path, a speed and an end relation, and it reveals, compares,
energises or resolves something. Avoid repeated push-ins, arbitrary orbits and focus pulls that make the
product unreadable. Light: direction, source size, shadow, separation and the material it reveals (glass
thickness, metal reflections, fabric weight).

## Progression and product presence

Opening, development and resolution in proportion to the length. Every important cut changes scale,
viewpoint, action, rhythm, colour or feeling. Hold key images long enough to recognise the product. Make the
product recognisable early (or make a delayed reveal earn its place) and keep it present through action or
motifs; do not spend most of the film on scenery and introduce the product at the end.

A 15-second three-shot example: macro texture reveal 0-5 s, product in hand or in use 5-10 s, hero product on
a surface with a slow push-in and space for the end frame 10-15 s.

## Sound

Design a brand-appropriate score: texture or motif, entry, build, resolution; leave space for product,
material and room sounds. Silence can carry a beat. Do not default to piano and strings, music on every
moment, or a whoosh on every cut. Speech, when present, stays intelligible. Put all of this in the prompt:
the Buildary MCP has no separate music or mixing tool.

## The ending

Design a final image that completes this idea: the product arrangement, a readable moving product, or a
product-led composition inside the established world. Budget time for recognition and any reading. Brand
name, slogan and call to action are choices from the brief, not automatic. Generated logos and small type are
unreliable: leave clean space for an end card when exact type matters, and in `9:16` keep it in the upper or
middle third (platform buttons cover the bottom fifth).
